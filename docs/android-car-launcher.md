# What is an Android car launcher?

An Android car launcher is a home screen app made for a car's dashboard screen. This page explains launchers and overlays on Android head units in general terms. It makes no claims about the software in any specific car, the Omoda 5 included.

## The launcher is the home screen

On Android, the launcher is the app you land on when you press Home. It shows your apps and widgets and starts whatever you tap. Android treats it as a regular app that has declared it can act as the home screen. When more than one such app is installed, Android lets you choose which one is the default.

That is why launchers can be replaced on phones and tablets. The same mechanism exists on any device that runs Android, as long as the manufacturer has left it available.

## What changes in a car

A phone launcher assumes a small portrait screen held in the hand. A dashboard screen is usually wide, fixed in place and used at arm's length. An Android car launcher is a launcher laid out for that setting. Typical choices are a landscape layout, large touch targets, and a home screen that keeps things like navigation and music within reach.

Those are design habits of the category. Each launcher decides its own layout.

## What an Android head unit is

A head unit is the screen and computer in the dashboard that handles media, navigation and settings. An Android head unit is one that runs Android itself. Many aftermarket units do, and some car makers build their factory systems on Android too.

What an owner can do on such a unit varies a lot. The manufacturer decides whether apps can be installed, whether the default home app can be changed and which permissions are open. An Android head unit launcher only works where the system allows a different home app.

## What an overlay is

An overlay is a window that an app draws on top of other apps. Floating chat bubbles and floating widgets are common examples on phones. Android guards this behind a permission, shown in settings as "Display over other apps", which the user has to grant.

In a car, an overlay can keep a small piece of information or a control visible while another app fills the screen. Whether that is possible depends again on what the head unit's software permits.

## Not the same as Android Auto

People often search for an "Android Auto launcher" when they mean a home screen for a car display, so the terms are worth separating.

- **Android Auto** runs on your phone and projects its interface onto the car's screen. The apps live on the phone.
- **Android Automotive OS** is a version of Android that runs in the car itself, built for vehicles.
- **A launcher on an Android head unit** is an app installed on the unit. It replaces the home screen of that unit.

A launcher installed on the head unit does not change what Android Auto shows, because Android Auto draws its screens from the phone.

## Before changing a head unit

Changing the software on a car's screen is your own responsibility. Check what the manufacturer allows, and install apps only from sources you trust. Set things up while parked.

## Where Omodroid fits

Omodroid is a planned Android launcher and overlay for one head unit, the Omoda 5 (2023) infotainment system. It is at an early stage with no code and no release, so nothing on this page describes how it works. The [project status page](/status) says what exists today and how to follow it.
