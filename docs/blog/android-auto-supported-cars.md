# How to check whether your car supports Android Auto

<p class="post-meta">By <a href="https://halit.org">Halit Sever</a> · <time datetime="2026-11-28">28 November 2026</time></p>

<p class="lead">No blog list of Android Auto cars stays correct for long. Google keeps the official list, car makers confirm the details, and your phone and country have to qualify too. This post shows where to look and what each source can and cannot tell you.</p>

## Step 1: Google's list

Google's [compatible cars and stereos list](https://www.android.com/auto/compatibility/) is the starting point. It lists car brands with their models and years, plus aftermarket stereos. If your car is there, it supports Android Auto in at least some markets and trims.

What the list does not answer:

- **Wired or wireless.** Google's requirements page says to ask the car maker to confirm wireless support for your model.
- **Your trim and market.** Equipment differs between countries and model years.
- **Brands Google has not listed.** A missing brand is a reason to ask the maker, not proof that the car lacks Android Auto.

## Step 2: the maker's own documents

The brochure, spec sheet or owner's manual for your exact model year is the best source. Look for "Android Auto" and whether it says wired, wireless or both.

An example from this site's topic: when this post was written, Google's list did not include Chery or Omoda, while [Chery Australia's OMODA 5 brochure (MY24, January 2025)](https://cherymotor.com.au/sites/default/files/2025-01/OMODA-5-Brochure-MY24-JAN2025.pdf) lists "Apple CarPlay & Android Auto (Wired + Wireless)" as equipment on the OMODA 5 sold in Australia. Both can be true at once; it shows why the maker's document for your market matters. Owners elsewhere should check their own country's brochure or dealer.

## Step 3: your phone

The car is only half. Google's [Android Auto requirements page](https://support.google.com/androidauto/answer/6348019) lists:

- Wired: Android 11.0 or higher with an active data plan. Some phone models are not supported.
- Wireless: a compatible Android phone with an active data plan and 5 GHz Wi-Fi support.

## Step 4: your country

The same page lists the countries where Android Auto is available, and says most features won't work outside them. The list includes Turkey, for example.

## Step 5: try it

With the car in park and the screen on, connect with a short data cable. Google's [Android Auto setup page](https://support.google.com/androidauto/answer/6348029) says to follow the prompts on the screen and that the phone may ask you to update Android Auto first. If it does not start, the [Android Auto troubleshooting checklist](/blog/android-auto-not-working) follows Google's steps in order.

## If your car does not support it

- An **aftermarket stereo** that supports Android Auto replaces the factory unit. Google's list includes aftermarket stereos.
- If the car supports only wired Android Auto, a [wireless adapter](/blog/wireless-android-auto-adapter) can remove the cable.
- If the car's screen runs Android itself, apps may run on the unit directly. That is a different thing from Android Auto, explained in [Android Auto vs a head unit vs a launcher](/blog/android-auto-vs-head-unit).

## FAQ

### Where is the official list of Android Auto cars?

Google's compatibility page at android.com/auto/compatibility lists car brands and models, and aftermarket stereos.

### Does a car on the list support wireless Android Auto?

Not necessarily. Google's requirements page says to ask the car manufacturer to confirm wireless support for your model.

### Is Android Auto available in Turkey?

Google's requirements page includes Turkey in its list of countries where Android Auto is available.
