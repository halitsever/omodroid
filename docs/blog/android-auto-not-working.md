# Android Auto not working or not connecting: a checklist

<p class="post-meta">By <a href="https://halit.org">Halit Sever</a> · <time datetime="2026-11-28">28 November 2026</time></p>

<p class="lead">When Android Auto will not start, the cause is almost always one of four things: the phone, the cable, the car or the wireless pairing. This checklist follows Google's own Android Auto help pages in that order. It contains no car-specific tricks, because those depend on the car maker.</p>

## 1. Check the phone

Google's [Android Auto requirements page](https://support.google.com/androidauto/answer/6348019) sets the minimum:

- **Wired:** an Android phone with Android 11.0 or higher and an active data plan. Some phone models are not supported.
- **Wireless:** a compatible Android phone with an active data plan and 5 GHz Wi-Fi support.

Install the latest Android update and update the Android Auto app from Google Play. Google's [Android Auto setup page](https://support.google.com/androidauto/answer/6348029) says the phone may ask you to update Android Auto the first time you connect. Keep the phone in mobile data range during setup.

## 2. Check that the car supports it

A USB port is not enough. Google's [Android Auto troubleshooting page](https://support.google.com/androidauto/answer/6348190) says Android Auto won't work on all cars equipped with a USB port. Look your car or stereo up on Google's [compatible cars and stereos list](https://www.android.com/auto/compatibility/), or ask the manufacturer. For wireless, the car itself has to support wireless Android Auto. The [supported cars post](/blog/android-auto-supported-cars) explains how to read that list.

Android Auto is also limited by country. Google's requirements page lists the countries where it is available and says most features won't work outside them.

## 3. Check the USB cable

This is the step Google is most specific about:

- If Android Auto worked before and stopped, replace the USB cable. Google says that will likely fix it.
- Use a cable shorter than 1 metre (3 feet).
- Do not use USB hubs or extension cables.
- Try the cable that came with your phone. Other cables can work if they meet USB Implementers Forum standards and were certified in the past two years.

Plug into the car's USB port that is meant for data. Many cars have charge-only ports, and Chery's own brochures, for example, mark which ports carry data.

## 4. Check the car's infotainment system

From the same [Android Auto troubleshooting page](https://support.google.com/androidauto/answer/6348190):

1. Make sure Android Auto is turned on in the car's infotainment settings.
2. Restart the infotainment system.
3. With an aftermarket receiver, such as a Pioneer or Kenwood unit, check the maker's website for a firmware update.

Google's setup page also says to start the wired connection with the car in park and the infotainment system on.

## 5. Reset the connection to this car

If the phone has been connected to other cars, Google suggests clearing the list:

1. Unplug the phone.
2. Open **Settings**, then **Connected devices**, **Connection preferences**, **Android Auto**.
3. Open **Previously connected cars**, tap the menu in the top right corner and choose **Forget all cars**.
4. Plug the phone in again.

## 6. Wireless: redo the first pairing

The first wireless connection needs a Bluetooth pairing between the phone and the car. Google's setup page says to keep Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and Location Services on while you pair, and that in many cars you open the pairing menu by pressing and holding the voice command button on the steering wheel. After pairing, Android Auto should start within a few seconds, or you may need to tap its icon on the car's screen.

If wireless keeps failing, check whether your car supports wireless Android Auto at all. Cars that only support the wired version are the reason [wireless adapters](/blog/wireless-android-auto-adapter) exist.

## When none of this helps

If the car is on Google's list, the phone meets the requirements and a short, known-good cable does not work, the next step is the car maker or the stereo maker. Google's help pages send aftermarket owners to the manufacturer for firmware, and car owners to the dealer.

Omodroid, the project behind this site, is a planned launcher for one car's head unit and has nothing to do with fixing Android Auto. The difference between the two is explained in [Android Auto vs a head unit vs a launcher](/blog/android-auto-vs-head-unit).

## FAQ

### Which Android version does Android Auto need?

Google's Android Auto requirements page lists Android 11.0 or higher with an active data plan for a wired connection, and a compatible phone with 5 GHz Wi-Fi for wireless.

### Why did Android Auto stop working when it worked before?

Google's troubleshooting page says that if Android Auto worked properly before and no longer does, replacing the USB cable will likely fix it.

### How do I reset Android Auto for one car?

Unplug the phone, open Settings, then Connected devices, Connection preferences, Android Auto, Previously connected cars, open the menu and choose Forget all cars. Then plug the phone in again.
